AKI is diagnosed by a creatinine rise of ≥ 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours, a rise to ≥ 1.5 times baseline within 7 days, or oliguria. According to the KDIGO system, AKI is classified into three stages based largely on creatinine[^creatinine] levels and urine output. AKI can impair the kidney’s ability to maintain electrolyte, acid–base, and water balance.

| Stage | Serum creatinine level | Urine output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1.5–1.9 times baseline or an increase of ≥ 0.3 mg/dL (≥ 26.5 μmol/L) | < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6–12 hours |
| 2 | 2.0–2.9 times baseline | < 0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥ 12 hours |
| 3 | ≥ 3.0 times baseline; an increase in serum creatinine to ≥ 4.0 mg/dL (≥ 353.6 μmol/L); or initiation of renal replacement therapy. In patients < 18 years, a decrease in eGFR to < 35 mL/min/1.73 m² also meets stage 3 criteria. | < 0.3 mL/kg/h for ≥ 24 hours or anuria for ≥ 12 hours |

This table is presented in Mercado et al.[^mercado] These are generic manifestations of the broader derangement of kidney function called AKI and do not identify specific physiological mechanisms or etiologies. The paper divides AKI etiology into three categories: prerenal, intrinsic renal, and postrenal. These terms do not refer to time; rather, they describe anatomical and physiological relationships to the kidney.

Intravascular volume depletion[^management] decreases venous return to the heart and can therefore lower cardiac output (CO). Because hydrostatic pressure drives filtration within the glomerulus, **prerenal** AKI is often caused by volume depletion or reduced renal perfusion. Fluid loss or fluid redistribution[^edema] may be involved. Prerenal causes arise before the renal parenchyma is injured; thus, they are termed *prerenal*.

Damage to the glomeruli or tubules themselves is a main cause of **intrinsic renal** pathology. Examples include immune-complex glomerular disease associated with systemic illness,[^immune-complex] acute tubular necrosis (ATN), and acute interstitial nephritis (AIN).[^nephritic-vs-nephrotic] ATN may result from ischemic or nephrotoxic injury. Ischemic injury may follow hypovolemia or hypoperfusion, whereas nephrotoxic injury may result from endogenous or exogenous toxins. AIN can be caused by a hypersensitivity reaction to certain medications.[^inflammatory-medications]

Postrenal etiologies consist of obstructions to urine flow, such as cervical cancer.

[^creatinine]: Creatine and phosphocreatine in skeletal muscle participate in rapid ATP regeneration. Creatinine is a nonenzymatic breakdown product of creatine and phosphocreatine; it is not a protein. It is filtered through the glomerulus and excreted in urine, with a small amount of tubular secretion. A rising serum creatinine concentration generally indicates reduced glomerular filtration and impaired kidney function, although it is an imperfect and delayed marker of acute changes in GFR.

[^mercado]: Mercado, M. G., Smith, D. K., & Guard, E. L. (2019). *Acute Kidney Injury: Diagnosis and Management*. *American Family Physician, 100*(11), 687–694.

[^management]: Fluid resuscitation may be indicated to correct intravascular volume depletion. Isotonic crystalloids are recommended over colloids because they are inexpensive, effective, and generally safer. Colloids have a theoretical advantage because their larger molecules should be less likely to leave the intravascular space. However, they do not consistently improve clinical outcomes, and some synthetic colloids can cause kidney injury.

[^edema]: Nephrotic syndrome may lead to urinary loss of albumin, resulting in reduced plasma albumin. Reduced oncotic pressure promotes edema by allowing fluid to accumulate in the interstitial tissues. In some cases, this can reduce effective arterial blood volume and contribute to prerenal AKI, although edema does not invariably indicate intravascular volume depletion.

[^nephritic-vs-nephrotic]: Nephritic and nephrotic syndromes are distinct. Nephritic syndrome is largely inflammatory in nature, whereas nephrotic syndrome results from a variety of abnormalities that disrupt the glomerular filtration barrier and cause substantial protein loss. AKI is not a specific disease; it describes a rapid decline in kidney function. Nephritic and nephrotic syndromes describe sets of clinical manifestations. Glomerulonephritis (GN) is a general term for diseases that inflame and damage the glomeruli.

[^inflammatory-medications]: These drugs are not inherently inflammatory, but some may act as haptens: small molecules that become immunogenic after binding host proteins. This can trigger a hypersensitivity reaction that injures the renal interstitium and nearby tubules.

[^immune-complex]: Deposition of circulating immune complexes may involve endogenous or exogenous antigens and can recruit complement-mediated leukocyte activation. Classically, complexes containing endogenous antigens occur in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). SLE is a typical example of autoimmunity caused by loss of self-tolerance. Patients make antibodies against their own nuclear material. These antibodies bind circulating or tissue-associated self-antigens, forming antigen–antibody complexes that activate complement and perpetuate tissue damage.
